# Generation and expansion guide

Read this when preparing either image-generation stage or developing a larger body of work.

## Preserve lineage

Maintain a simple lineage across stages:

`source image + user trace -> aesthetic seed -> concept image -> new artwork -> body of work`

At each transition, state internally what must survive and what may mutate. The invariant should be a relationship, perceptual operation, or generative rule rather than a list of source objects.

## Concept-image stage

Purpose: make the extracted essence visible enough to test.

Possible modes:

- **Structural map:** shows how boundary, material, movement, and space interact.
- **Material experiment:** removes narrative and tests a material contradiction.
- **Visual essay:** uses layers, fragments, or repetitions to expose an idea.
- **Abstract embryo:** retains only the most fertile visual law.

Default to the abstract embryo only when the user has no preference. Do not rely on labels or explanatory text inside the image unless requested. The concept image can be beautiful, but its job is diagnosis and discovery, not final polish.

Before generation, confirm:

- exact pixel dimensions or aspect ratio;
- intended display or production use;
- analytical-to-poetic balance;
- desired recognizability of the source;
- any content that must not appear.

## New-artwork stage

Purpose: let the seed acquire a new body.

Offer distance choices in plain language:

- **Close kin:** the source remains perceptible, but the result stands alone.
- **Translated growth:** the same rule moves into new objects, materials, or space.
- **Distant growth:** only the emotional and philosophical structure remains recognizable.

Choose styles and media by functional fit. Explain why each could carry the seed. A medium suggestion should describe what it contributes—for example, translucent sculpture can make an unstable boundary physical—rather than merely naming an art movement.

Confirm dimensions again even if the user already chose dimensions for the concept image. Then generate from the updated seed and concept image. Avoid generic surrealism, decorative texture transfer, and literal symbol substitution unless the user intentionally chooses them.

## Expansion into a body of work

First identify what the new artwork added to the seed. Then propose a few developments with distinct reasons, not an exhaustive catalog.

Confirm:

- form: series, object, space, book, motion, sound, performance, or hybrid;
- number of works or components;
- dimensions and output context;
- shared invariants;
- controlled variables that change between pieces;
- sequence, rhythm, or visitor/user experience;
- production realism versus speculative concept freedom.

For a series, define a variation grammar. Example: preserve “hard boundaries behaving sensitively” while varying scale, material, degree of disturbance, and distance from the viewer. Do not create near-duplicates.

For an exhibition, develop a perceptual journey rather than a list of objects. For a product or object, retain the seed through behavior, material, interaction, or construction—not merely surface graphics.

## Image prompt construction

Build prompts from:

1. confirmed proposition and emotional temperature;
2. invariant visual relationship;
3. chosen degree of distance;
4. suitable medium and material behavior;
5. composition, light, space, and scale;
6. exclusions that prevent literal or clichéd drift;
7. requested dimensions or aspect ratio.

Do not expose a long technical prompt unless the user asks. Present the artistic intent in readable language and show the generated result.
